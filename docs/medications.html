ACM EXTENDED / REFERENCE

Medication reference

Indications, doses, timing, adverse effects and interactions for ACM Extended medications, with unavailable legacy entries clearly marked.

Reading the cards

These cards describe game behavior. Vial pictures reproduce the mod’s artwork; use the written concentration on each card when a printed vial label differs. Dose gives the in game reference amount or fixed product action, with separate examples where needed. A reference dose is not a maximum dose, a whole vial, or a promise of a specific response. Weight based examples use the game’s 83 kg reference patient. Hover, tap or use the graph time control to inspect the curve. Readouts show the delivered reference dose and relative game effect, not a measured serum concentration. A hollow point marks an isolated boundary value in the native timing formula. Timings begin after medication reaches the patient; a closed line, required flush or poor IM absorption changes what you observe.

Adenosine12 mg/4 mL
Indications
Regular SVT with a pulse.
Contraindications
Does not terminate AFib, atrial tachycardia, VT, VF, PEA or asystole. Avoid using it to treat a fast sinus rate.
Route
IV / IO push.
Dose
6 mg is the game reference dose (2 mL of the 3 mg/mL vial). A 6 mg dose delivered over 5 seconds meets the modeled rapid push requirement; slower delivery weakens conversion.
Onset
AV-nodal slowing builds over about 1 second after the dose reaches the patient. The SVT conversion check occurs at about 1 second.
Peak effect
  • IV / IO1 s
Side effects
Brief slowing of the heart rate. The dedicated effect lasts less than 15 seconds. It does not deliberately create a flatline or cardiac arrest.
Special considerations
With Require saline flush after push enabled, complete the flush before judging the response. Hanging a slow adenosine infusion does not reproduce a rapid push.
Medication interactions
No separate drug pair conversion bonus is modeled. Other rate lowering drugs can still influence the patient's final heart rate.

Amiodarone150 mg/3 mL
Indications
Improves defibrillation response in VF and pulseless VT. Rate controls AFib with RVR and can terminate atrial tachycardia or SVT; its reviewed perfusing conversion path does not directly convert VT.
Contraindications
Does not treat torsades. Avoid excessive cumulative dosing or a rapid infusion, especially when blood pressure is already low.
Route
IV / IO push or prepared saline infusion.
Dose
150 mg reference dose (3 mL). For a controlled infusion, 150 mg over 10 minutes delivers 15 mg/min. Stock vial: 150 mg.
Onset
Builds after systemic delivery; the reference effect peaks at 10 seconds. Rhythm conversion can occur before the peak once enough effect is present.
Peak effect
  • IV / IO10 s
Side effects
Hypotension with excessive delivery rate; nausea. Large exposure can cause severe bradycardia, and cumulative exposure of 2.2 g or more can contribute to torsades.
Special considerations
Require saline flush after push applies when enabled. A 150 mg reference is a model amount, not a maximum cumulative dose or a guaranteed conversion.
Medication interactions
Esmolol adds further slowing and hypotension. Ondansetron can reduce medication related nausea. Hyaluronidase is the matching local treatment for an extravasation injury.

Atropine1 mg/1 mL
Indications
Raises heart rate; also treats the modeled effects of sarin exposure once sufficient active medication is present.
Contraindications
Avoid treating an already fast rhythm with a drug that raises rate. It does not directly terminate a tachyarrhythmia or provide ROSC.
Route
IV / IO or IM.
Dose
Reference dose: 1 mg IV / IO or 2 mg IM. Sarin treatment depends on accumulated active effect, so one reference dose is not a complete toxin reversal.
Onset
Effect develops after delivery. IV / IO peaks at 5 seconds; IM peaks at 30 seconds. Chemical buildup reduction is gradual.
Peak effect
  • IV / IO5 s
  • IM30 s
Side effects
Increased heart rate. Excess active medication can produce a larger than intended rate response.
Special considerations
CBRN treatment can also clear sarin related airway spasm at sufficient effect. Airway and ventilation support may still be needed.
Medication interactions
No dedicated interaction with esmolol or amiodarone is modeled; their rate effects still contribute to the final vital signs. Atropine and dimercaprol treat different chemical agents.

Calcium chloride1g/10 mL
Indications
Calcium replacement during blood product resuscitation, impaired clotting and hypocalcemia. Helps correct a hypocalcemic cause of recurrent torsades.
Contraindications
Use cautiously when calcium is already adequate. Excessive amount or delivery rate can cause severe toxicity. It does not replace volume or magnesium for active torsades.
Route
IV / IO push or prepared saline infusion.
Dose
1 g reference dose (10 mL of 100 mg/mL). This contains 273 mg of elemental calcium in the game's calcium calculation.
Onset
Admitted dose starts contributing to calcium replacement during delivery. The reference medication effect peaks at 5 minutes.
Peak effect
  • IV / IO5 min
Side effects
Extravasation injury. Excess calcium can first raise blood pressure, then cause bradycardia, low blood pressure and arrest as toxicity worsens.
Special considerations
Require saline flush after push applies when enabled. Delivery speed matters: equal total doses given at different rates can have different toxicity.
Medication interactions
Calcium gluconate adds to the same elemental calcium burden. Correcting low calcium improves responsiveness to epinephrine and norepinephrine. Hyaluronidase treats the local extravasation injury.

Epinephrine1 mg/1 mL
Indications
Measured push dose blood pressure support, pressor infusion and cardiac arrest medication support. The IM formulation has a separate vital sign effect.
Contraindications
Excessive rate or repeated dosing can cause tachycardia and a pressor surge. It does not correct blood loss; hypovolemia requires volume replacement.
Route
IV / IO or IM; permitted in prepared saline infusions. The dedicated Push Dose Epinephrine action is separate.
Dose
Reference: 1 mg IV / IO or 0.3 mg IM. These are not push dose amounts. The dedicated measured push dose action uses micrograms.
Onset
IV reference peak: 5 seconds. IM peak: 30 seconds. Infusion blood pressure support ramps in progressively and fades after stopping.
Peak effect
  • IV / IO5 s
  • IM30 s
Side effects
Tachycardia, blood pressure overshoot, atrial tachycardia or SVT after a sustained surge, and vasopressor extravasation injury.
Special considerations
The regular vial is 1 mg/mL. The cardiac vial is ten times more dilute. Allow Push Dose Epinephrine Action and Dedicated Bolus Actions: Required Training govern the dedicated action.
Medication interactions
Low calcium and acidosis reduce pressor effectiveness. Other pressors add blood pressure support. Phentolamine treats local epinephrine extravasation. Sedatives may oppose its pressure effect without correcting the underlying shock.

Esmolol100 mg/10 mL
Indications
Rate controls AFib with RVR; can terminate atrial tachycardia or SVT. Also slows sinus heart rate.
Contraindications
Avoid in an already slow or poorly perfusing patient. It does not replace defibrillation for VF, pulseless VT or torsades.
Route
IV / IO. Available as a vial and a premixed infusion.
Dose
0.5 mg/kg reference dose, equivalent to 41.5 mg at 83 kg. Premixed bag contains 2.5 g in 250 mL; bag contents are not a single bolus dose.
Onset
Reference peak: 20 seconds. Rate control occurs once enough delivered medication has taken effect, not simply when the bag is connected.
Peak effect
  • IV / IO20 s
Side effects
Excess exposure can cause marked bradycardia and hypotension. An extravasated infusion can injure the site.
Special considerations
A controlled AFib response remains AFib, usually near a 90 BPM target. Effect continues to wash out after the infusion is stopped.
Medication interactions
Amiodarone adds further slowing and hypotension. Esmolol reduces lidocaine clearance, increasing toxicity risk. Hyaluronidase treats matching extravasation.

Hyaluronidase150U/1 mLShared mod icon. Read the card name and dose; the printed vial label belongs to norepinephrine.
Indications
Local treatment of extravasation from calcium, amiodarone, magnesium, esmolol, hypertonic saline or mannitol.
Contraindications
Does not treat epinephrine/norepinephrine extravasation. Injecting systemically does not substitute for the local infiltration treatment.
Route
Local infiltration at the affected body part.
Dose
150 units is a full reference infiltration (1 mL). Smaller infiltrations can add together within the 90-second treatment window.
Onset
Recovery support starts when a matching local infiltration is completed. Tissue healing remains gradual.
Peak effect
Local recovery treatment; no fixed systemic peak.
Side effects
No separate systemic adverse syndrome is modeled for the local action. Choosing the wrong antidote leaves the injury untreated.
Special considerations
Each infiltration treats one matching exposure, prioritizing the most advanced injury and then the most recent exposure. Check the line and stop further leakage.
Medication interactions
Matches the listed irritant drugs. Phentolamine is the other local antidote and treats vasopressor leaks instead.
Hypertonic saline 3%3% / 250 mL premixed bag
Indications
Osmotherapy for raised ICP in a patient with modeled TBI.
Contraindications
No useful ICP reduction when pressure is already at its treatment floor. Further osmotherapy is blocked at the Sodium ceiling, including mannitol in the current shared treatment check.
Route
IV / IO infusion from the 250 mL premixed bag.
Dose
250 mL is the osmotherapy reference amount. A full bag contains 7.5 g of sodium chloride.
Onset
Acts progressively as fluid reaches the patient. There is no separate fixed peak time for a whole infusion.
Peak effect
Depends on the amount and rate delivered.
Side effects
Increases the modeled sodium level. Extravasation can damage the access site.
Special considerations
Rate and delivered volume determine the response. The 23.4% bullet is a separate product and action, described below. Sodium ceiling (mEq/L) is under ACM Extended: Neuro & TBI.
Medication interactions
Mannitol shares the osmotherapy limit. Positioning and circulation support can also change ICP/CPP. Hyaluronidase treats matching extravasation.
Ketamine500 mg/10 mL
Indications
Analgesia at smaller doses and dose dependent sedation for procedures or intubation.
Contraindications
Severe hypovolemia/acidosis can reduce its usual blood pressure support. Large or fast IV doses and sedative combinations can depress breathing.
Route
IV / IO or IM; permitted in prepared saline infusions.
Dose
Analgesia reference: 0.2 mg/kg IV / IO or 0.75 mg/kg IM. At the default induction threshold, ketamine alone reaches the AI induction level at about 1.75 mg/kg IV or 4.375 mg/kg IM at full effect. The current IM calibration uses less hypnotic effect per dose than earlier versions.
Onset
Reference peak: 5 seconds IV / IO or 20 seconds IM. Sedation begins only when the combined active dose crosses the induction threshold.
Peak effect
  • IV / IO5 s
  • IM20 s
Side effects
Nausea; dose dependent heart rate and pressure changes. Rapid/high exposure can depress breathing, and overdose can cause severe respiratory depression.
Special considerations

Ketamine sedation and nystagmus tips are in Ventilator Settings & Tips.

Ketamine: induction threshold and Ketamine: maintenance threshold are under ACM Extended: Airway. Ketamine continues to use its detailed effect model even when ACE uses basic medication behavior.
Medication interactions
Propofol and midazolam add to hypnosis. Fentanyl/morphine augment sedation and respiratory depression; midazolam adds another respiratory interaction. Rocuronium adds paralysis without adding sedation. Ondansetron reduces nausea.
