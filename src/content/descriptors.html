Clinical descriptors

Find the exact examination wording, the state that selects it, and the difference made by the Clinical Descriptors setting.

Reading an examination

The descriptor setting changes wording. Check Breathing, Feel Skin, Assess Pupils and fracture inspection each select findings through their own rules. An assessment label is one observation; read it with the rest of the patient assessment.

01

Breathing findings

The first matching condition determines the result. A driving ventilator is checked first, then an active irregular breathing pattern, then the ordinary breathing assessment.

PriorityConditionReported finding
1Ventilator is driving the living patientMechanical ventilation with delivered rate and exhaled tidal volume. VTe below 50 mL or delivered minute volume below 0.2 L/min reports minimal effective exhaled volume.
2Irregular breathing pattern is activeBreathing is irregular in rate and depth.
3RR below 1, HR below 20, death, tension pneumothorax, hemothorax fluid above 1.4, or blocked airwayNot breathing / apneic
4A shallow breathing condition is presentShallow, with slow or rapid wording added according to the rate thresholds below.
5No higher condition appliesRate alone selects slow, normal or rapid.

Shallow breathing conditions include pneumothorax, an unsecured collapsed airway, absent airway reflexes without an adjunct or securing maneuver, airway inflammation above 15, or lung tissue damage above 20.

Rate in breaths/minOrdinary depthShallow condition present
Below 15.9Patient is bradypneicPatient is bradypneic with poor tidal volume
15.9 through 22Patient is eupneic with equal chest risePatient is breathing with poor tidal volume
Above 22Patient is tachypneicPatient is tachypneic with shallow respirations

Apneic means no breathing is reported. Bradypneic means slow; tachypneic means fast; eupneic is the normal depth, normal rate wording. Poor tidal volume describes shallow breaths. The mechanically ventilated result uses delivered measurements instead of these spontaneous breathing labels.

Skin findings

The displayed color uses whichever is worse: perfusion tier or temperature band. Temperature bands are below 32°C, below 34°C, below 36°C, and 36°C or above. Clinical Descriptors changes clammy to diaphoretic; the live skin examination does not use every generic skin entry in the wording catalog below.

03

Pupils

StatePlain wordingClinical wording
Normal state, ICP below the early threshold, no Cushing flagBoth pupils are equal and react normally to light.Pupils: PERRL.
Normal state with ICP at or above the early threshold, or Cushing flagBoth pupils are equal but react slowly.Pupils: equal/round, sluggish bilaterally.
Pupil state 1The affected pupil is bigger and slower than the other.Affected side sluggish/dilating, other side brisk. Anisocoria.
Pupil state 2The affected pupil is wide open and does not react. The other still does.Affected side blown (fixed, dilated), other side reactive.
Pupil state 3Both pupils are wide open and neither reacts to light.Pupils: bilateral fixed + dilated.

The early ICP threshold defaults to 18. Side specific text follows the injury side. PERRL means pupils equal, round and reactive to light. Anisocoria means unequal pupil size.

Nystagmus may be appended when the patient is alive, unconscious, outside arrest, not paralyzed, and the induction normalized ketamine effect is at least 1 and exceeds propofol plus midazolam. Plain wording reports rapid involuntary eye movement. This is an additional finding and does not replace the pupil examination.

04

Capillary refill

The live ACM check reports a time band. It starts at 4, adjusts for blood volume when a pulse is present, and applies a MAP adjustment when blood volume is above 5 L. An arm tourniquet prevents the arm specific volume adjustment.

Computed refill valueDisplayed band
Below 2~2 seconds
2 to below 3<3 seconds
3 to below 4~3 seconds
4 or above>4 seconds

The band labels are the literal game output, including the final “>4” label at a computed value of exactly 4. The arm calculation maps 5.8 to 5.2 L across 2 to 4 seconds; the torso calculation maps 5.5 to 4.8 L across the same range. These volume conversions are not clamped. The generic wording catalog does not replace this live numeric result.

05

Fracture inspection

Clinical fracture inspection evaluates the selected limb in this order. Earlier rows take priority over later rows.

Priority / stateClinical log finding
1. Splint presentSplint in place; crepitus not assessed
2. ACE fracture state is treated (-1)Limb stabilized; crepitus not reassessed
3. Complex fracture (3)Severe contusion, marked swelling and deformity; crepitus present
4. Severe fracture (2)Severe contusion and swelling; crepitus present
5. Mild fracture (1)Severe contusion; no crepitus found
6. Unexpected fracture state or remaining positive ACE fractureLimb findings indeterminate; reassess
7. Body part damage above 1Visible contusion; no crepitus found
8. None of the aboveNo visible contusion or deformity; no crepitus found

Crepitus is the grinding finding used by the game’s presentation rules. No crepitus found does not exclude a fracture: the mild fracture row explicitly uses that wording. Previous realignment is appended to the finding and does not clear severity. With Clinical Descriptors disabled, the native ACM inspection provides the result.

The response sentences include the patient name. Agonal respirations are gasping; myoclonic activity describes involuntary jerking. Ecchymosis means bruising; pharyngeal edema means swelling in the throat. Mild and significant airway inflammation retain their native wording.
