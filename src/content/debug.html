ACM EXTENDED / MISSION TOOLS

Debug menu reference

Read the current TBI fields and the supplied debug screenshot, distinguish live patient values from diagnostic defaults, and identify which system needs checking.

Start with the patient and the units

The overlay exposes game state for testing and troubleshooting. It includes patient measurements, internal model scores, system switches and machine wide counters. A green value is a display classification; it does not establish that the patient is stable.

Enable Addon Options > ACM Extended: Debug > Overlay > Debug menu. This is a client side option. The same group contains the text scale setting and visibility switches for optional sections.

Current layout · TBI revision B119

The current overlay has CLINICAL 1/2 and NETWORK 2/2 pages, switched with Ctrl + Page Up / Page Down. The clinical page contains the new NEURO / TBI block. The image below records the earlier three column layout; its field explanations remain here for comparison. Current TBI meanings are documented in TBI, ICP and infusions.

Check the target

The name at the top identifies the patient being inspected. A treatment can change that target.

Read the whole label

Eff under Vitals is volume. Eff under Pressors is support. CPP under Acidosis is a deficit fraction, not a pressure.

Recognize defaults

n/a means unavailable. no TBI state was the earlier renderer’s explicit empty-state label. The current TBI block can show zero severity/ICP, integrity 1 and PerfOK yes as defaults when no state is populated.

03

TBI, ICP and infusions

The current NEURO / TBI block separates lasting injury, active burden and the two control systems. Read its trend alongside blood volume, oxygen delivery and breathing. A normal saturation alone does not establish that the brain receives enough oxygen.

Display fieldUnit / formatMeaning and interpretation
Acute0–1 score{{acute-severity|Active secondary injury burden}}, stored as severity. It can fall toward zero when pressure, oxygen delivery and ventilation are adequate. Past herniation can impose a recovery floor.
Struct0–1 score{{structural-severity|Lasting structural injury history}}, stored as structuralSeverity. This controls remaining reserve and severity based pressure requirements. A resolved concussion can retain Struct while Acute resolves; Struct alone no longer keeps producing raised ICP or unconsciousness.
ICPmmHg, roundedCurrent modeled pressure inside the skull. During recovery it approaches the normal baseline, usually 10 mmHg, rather than zero. Zero can be an empty-state fallback in this renderer.
CPPmmHg, roundedThis clinical display calculates systemic MAP − ICP. The treatment handler uses effective brain MAP − ICP: head elevation subtracts 6 mmHg from MAP by default. The displayed CPP can therefore be 6 mmHg higher than the value used by the recovery gate.
AutoregIntegrity, 0.05–1 after initialization{{cerebral-autoregulation|Cerebral autoregulation}}: how well the brain regulates its own blood flow as pressure changes. Lower integrity increases vulnerability to low CPP and makes high CPP more pressure passive, so excessive pressure can add to ICP. This is separate from systemic autonomic control.
AutoIntegrity / signed toneFirst value: {{autonomic-integrity|systemic autonomic integrity}}, bounded to 0.05–1 after initialization. Second: {{autonomic-tone|tone from −1 to +1}}. Positive means a tightening influence on vessels, near zero means neutral TBI tone, and negative means a dilating influence. The renderer does not print a plus sign on positive values.
Hernyes / noThe herniating state flag. It is not the numbered stage or the maximum stage reached; use the fuller state dump for progression, accumulated exposure and recovery floor.
Cushingyes / noRaised ICP has reached the Cushing trigger, normally 25 mmHg. The flag alone does not tell you whether vascular reserve remains intact. Autonomic failure can affect resistance without the full Cushing pulse and breathing pattern.
Obtundyes / noThe patient’s {{obtundation|reduced alertness}} flag. It is distinct from unconsciousness and is not a direct measure of structural injury.
PerfOKyes / noThe TBI pressure gate: effective MAP meets the structural grade’s recovery minimum, ICP is at most 25, and any grade specific CPP floor is met. Severe injury adds CPP ≥55; critical adds CPP ≥60 mmHg; mild/moderate do not get that added high CPP requirement. Oxygen delivery, saturation and ventilation must also pass before acute burden can resolve.
Colour is not the recovery rule

The compact CPP display still uses fixed colours: below 50 red, 50 to below 70 amber, and 70 or above green. Those colours do not encode the new structural grade or head elevation adjustment. PerfOK yes with an amber CPP can be consistent with recovery. Mild/moderate injury is no longer universally harmed below MAP 85; see the head injury pressure and recovery guide for the actual thresholds. PerfOK defaults to yes if the state is missing.

Watch integrity and tone together

Compensating

Auto 0.82 / 0.64

Positive tone supports vessel tightening. It can raise peripheral resistance and reduce the custom junctional bleeding channel. This is a controller example, not a guarantee of adequate blood flow.

Losing reserve

Integrity falls; tone swings.

The output becomes less reliable. Compare successive Auto readings with SVR, MAP, bleeding and PerfOK instead of treating a single high pressure as recovery.

Failing

Auto 0.30 / -0.70

Negative tone favours vasodilation. Falling systemic resistance and weaker local vessel compensation can allow junctional hemorrhage to accelerate.

Vascular failure can precede the full vital sign pattern. The resistance only path changes systemic vascular resistance without forcing Cushing bradycardia, abnormal respirations or a hypertensive target. It is not a separate electrical rhythm diagnosis. During established cardiac arrest, the TBI vital sign writer releases its outputs to the arrest system; the TBI state continues to update.

Junctional vessel compensation reads integrity as 0.25 + 0.75 × integrity of its normal ability. The formula spans 25% at zero to 100% at one; the current TBI controller’s 0.05 lower bound gives 28.75% at that bound. Signed tone is already carried through systemic resistance and is not multiplied into the junctional equation a second time.

05

Pressor response

Raw, Eff and Lost show the model’s estimated pressure support response. Compare support with the actual BP trend. Acidosis and cold can reduce the response even while medication is reaching the patient.

Display fieldUnit / formatMeaning and interpretation
RawmmHg equivalent supportCombined modeled infusion, bolus and surge support before the acidosis and cold reductions. Earlier delivery, volume and calcium gates may already have affected this number.
EffmmHg equivalent supportSupport remaining after the acidosis and cold reductions. This is not a fluid volume.
LostmmHg equivalent supportmax(Raw − Eff, 0). It measures modeled loss of response, not leaked drug mass.
Acid %% reductionAcidosis related support reduction. It is not blood pH, the total acidosis score or the percentage of the dose lost.
Cold %% reductionHypothermia related support reduction. Acid and Cold multiply their remaining fractions; simply adding their percentages overstates the combined loss.
InfDiagnostic support mirrorThe public infusion support mirror read by this overlay. The current detailed support is stored in the circulation state; a default zero in this mirror is not proof that no infusion is active.
PDEDiagnostic support mirrorPush dose epinephrine support mirror. PDE here means push dose epinephrine, not a phosphodiesterase medication. The current bolus support calculation is tracked separately in circulation state.
DistalLegacy surge mirrorFormer distal site pressor spike diagnostic. The current circulation calculation explicitly sets the location only spike to zero. A distal IV does not itself turn norepinephrine into epinephrine; local leakage and vesicant injury remain separate concerns.
Reading the calculation

Effective support = Raw × (1 − Acid fraction) × (1 − Cold fraction). For example, Raw 20 with Acid 25% and Cold 20% gives 20 × 0.75 × 0.80 = 12, with 8 lost. These are diagnostic support units, not a dosing recommendation.
