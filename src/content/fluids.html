Fluids & blood volume

Follow fluid from the bag into the blood, plasma and crystalloid compartments, then distinguish total circulating volume from gradual conversion.

After the fluid enters

The current volume function returns the combined retained blood, plasma and saline compartments to ACE. Plasma and crystalloid also convert gradually into the blood compartment while blood volume is below 6 L. Those are separate steps.

Only admitted volume receives systemic credit. Leakage can make admitted volume much smaller than the amount removed from the bag. The retained compartments are capped within the shared 6 L capacity, and excess fluid is handled by the overload model.

02

Conversion rates

When the blood compartment is below 6 L, plasma converts at 0.003 L/s and saline compartment fluid at 0.0007 L/s. The conversion modifier doubles when fresh blood effectiveness exceeds 0.83. Conversion is limited by the available fluid in each source compartment.

CompartmentOrdinary conversionWith doubled modifierIdeal time for 1 L at ordinary rate
Plasma180 mL/min360 mL/min5 min 33 s
Saline / crystalloid42 mL/min84 mL/min23 min 49 s

These times assume 1 L is already present, enough room remains in the blood compartment, and no ongoing losses, additional delivery or competing limits change the state. They are conversion times, not bag administration times.

Saturation improves while the patient remains unstableOxygen saturation and circulating volume describe different parts of the patient state

Use the volume trend and repeated assessments together. An emptied bag does not prove the same volume is retained or that all blood compartment findings have recovered.

04

Conservation at the IV site

Systemic medication dose = concentration × drained volume × admission fraction. The volume and medication calculations share the site’s admission fraction.

ExampleBag lossAdmittedLocal loss
Intact line100 mL100 mL0 mL
Compromised catheter100 mL20 mL80 mL
Compromised catheter plus native stage-2 infiltration100 mL16 mL84 mL

For a 1 mg/mL infusion, the final row corresponds to 16 mg systemic delivery and 84 mg local leakage. This is a calculation example, not a dosing target.
