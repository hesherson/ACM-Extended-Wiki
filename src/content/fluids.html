Fluids & blood volume

Follow fluid from the bag into the blood, plasma and crystalloid compartments, then distinguish total circulating volume from gradual conversion.

After the fluid enters

The current volume function returns the combined retained blood, plasma and saline compartments to ACE. Plasma and crystalloid also convert gradually into the blood compartment while blood volume is below 6 L. Those are separate steps.

02

Conversion rates

When the blood compartment is below 6 L, plasma converts at 0.003 L/s and saline compartment fluid at 0.0007 L/s. The conversion modifier doubles when fresh blood effectiveness exceeds 0.83. Conversion is limited by the available fluid in each source compartment.

Calcium during blood transfusion

ACME game reference. Start indicated replacement early and control the delivery rate. In these examples, one unit means 500 mL of compatible blood actually admitted to the patient.

First unit and fourth unit

First unit: begin during transfusion

If calcium has not already been supplied, start controlled replacement during the first unit. If that unit finishes untreated, begin promptly. There is no extra grace period after the bag empties: the native HR target penalty starts once the uncleared transfusion burden exceeds 50 mL. This does not mean a patient instantly collapses at 50 mL.

Fourth unit: reassess before or during it

Review earlier calcium, actual delivery and the ongoing blood rate as the fourth unit starts. Arrange further replacement when the accumulating burden and remaining credit require it. If no calcium has been given, do not wait for the fourth bag to finish. The code has no special fourth-unit trigger or mandatory extra dose at that bag number.

CautionPrompt treatment still needs controlled delivery

For the same replacement reference, use 1 g calcium chloride or 3 g calcium gluconate, accounting for calcium already given. Both salts contribute to the same toxicity accumulator. Active transfusion does not disable those separate toxicity checks. Giving both as separate full replacement doses doubles that intended replacement and adds their elemental loads.

Continue reassessment throughout later units. A bag count is a reminder to check calcium, not a measurement of the remaining deficit. Follow pulse/rhythm, pressure, bleeding and line function; the debug calcium values, when available, are game model values.

CautionThe 120-second gluconate default is not a full-dose safety limit

The documented Hardcore default is 300 s for chloride and 120 s for gluconate. But 3 g gluconate over 120 s = 139.5 mg/min elemental calcium, above the configured 100 mg/min fast-rate onset. For that full 3 g reference, a 300 s delivery gives 55.8 mg/min instead. Check the actual selected volume and concentration each time.

Rate and total exposure both matter

Fast-rate toxicity starts above 100 mg/min elemental calcium and reaches full rate severity at 280 mg/min. The separate excess accumulator starts causing toxicity above 1 mg/dL of modeled excess; 4 mg/dL reaches full excess severity. The worse effect applies. Those are excess-proxy values, not measured ionized calcium.

Do not use the mathematical boundary as a target

At the 100 mg/min elemental boundary, 1 g chloride takes 2.73 min and 3 g gluconate takes 2.79 min. That only addresses the rate term. It does not establish a safe correction duration, because accumulation and prior doses still apply. The five-minute examples produce lower sustained inputs, 54.6 and 55.8 mg/min.

The Hardcore syringe timer is limited to 300 s. For a longer delivery, use a controlled prepared infusion or deliberately separated measured portions. A diluted bag needs its own concentration and admitted-flow calculation; the undiluted mL/min values above do not transfer to it. See the drip calculator and slow push controls.

What an untreated transfusion changes

Early native HR target penalty

Above 0.05 L of uncleared transfusion burden, the native adjustment is minus burden in liters / 0.05 bpm. Exactly 50 mL does not pass the strict greater-than check. This adjustment feeds the HR update with other effects; it is not a promise that the displayed pulse immediately drops by that exact amount.

Additional ACME calcium deficit

By default, the additional circulation model starts accruing deficit above 1 L of remaining burden: 0.18 normalized calcium per extra liter, offset by 0.12 per gram of cumulative CaCl₂-equivalent credit. Low calcium adds bleeding amplification, pressure suppression and eventually reduced pressor response. These thresholds are separate from the early 50 mL HR rule.

The table assumes no calcium, no prior credit, no burden clearance and 500 mL actually admitted per unit. Pressure entries are the calcium model's suppression targets, applied gradually through circulation, not guaranteed changes on the cuff.

How long does correction take?

While both transfusion burden and native calcium credit are positive, the native loop removes burden at up to 0.005 L/s = 300 mL/min. Without that calcium credit, this loop has no passive clearance with time. A stopped blood bag therefore does not start a countdown that clears its burden by itself.

Existing burdenNo more blood enteringBlood continues at 100 mL/min
0.5 LAbout 1 min 40 sAbout 2 min 30 s to catch up
2.0 LAbout 6 min 40 sAbout 10 min to catch up

These arithmetic examples assume enough usable calcium credit throughout and ordinary ticking. They are burden-clearance times, not medication push times or guaranteed patient-recovery times. With incoming blood, the ideal net reduction is 300 minus admitted blood mL/min. At or above 300 mL/min combined admission, this clearance path cannot reduce an existing backlog while that input continues.

A warmer's nominal setting is 100 mL/min, but actual admission still depends on perfusion, line flow and leakage. At an actual 100 mL/min, an untreated line crosses the 50 mL threshold just after 30 seconds and delivers 500 mL in five minutes. Pressure recovery also eases over time, and the medication cards' 5- and 7-minute effect peaks are separate from burden clearance.

Why the next dose depends on reassessment

Native clearing credit

The first gram of CaCl₂-equivalent medication adds 2.5 native calcium-count units; later grams add 2 each, with a count cap of 3 on the native update. The first-gram bonus is spread across partial deliveries. Three grams of gluconate supplies approximately the same replacement credit as one gram of chloride.

Credit is not a fixed number of protected blood bags

While burden is positive, calcium count can be spent at 0.005 per second even when less than 0.005 L of burden is available to remove that tick. A full 2.5-count pool lasts about 8 min 20 s of continuous active clearing. Credit spent during administration reduces what remains afterward. Remaining credit, delivery timing, pauses and the blood rate change the result.

The separate ACME CaCl₂-equivalent total contributes to the normalized calcium calculation; it is not the same variable as the spendable native count or the toxicity excess accumulator. Check the response and cumulative amount before repeating, especially if changing salts. On continued transfusion, a first/fourth-unit reminder is useful for reassessment, but it cannot guarantee adequate replacement or rule out excessive calcium.

Main, Hardcore and actual delivery

In gameCheck the mod build and mode

The reviewed main revision 5416332 uses the recorded manual delivery duration for calcium's custom rate input. The reviewed dev revision a9ae90c includes Hardcore timing: with Hardcore Medications on, the selected duration drives delivery; with it off, the custom bolus rate input uses at least 300 s for chloride and 180 s for gluconate. These internal normal-mode windows are not visible administration timers, and do not guarantee that any amount is safe. Actual infusion-rate and accumulation effects remain active.

Replacement credit starts with medication admitted to the patient. A prepared syringe, leaked dose or dose parked in a flush-required line is not delivered replacement. Check the selected site and flush when required. Persistent Hardcore pushes and their flush handling are explained in the IV access guide.
