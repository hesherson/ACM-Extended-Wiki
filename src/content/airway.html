Suction controls and sweep

Open Use ACCUVAC or Use Suction Bag from the airway actions. Your carried equipment selects the device, with ACCUVAC taking priority when you carry both. Keep the suction tip inside the mouth and watch the fluid clear.

ACCUVAC

  1. Take the tool. In the suction screen it starts in your hand. During laryngoscopy, select its tray slot.
  2. Hold Left mouse and sweep. Move side to side inside the mouth. Direction changes count as a sweep; holding the tip still stops effective clearance.
  3. Release and reassess. Confirm that the airway fluid clears and check oxygenation. Continuous suction has a separate oxygenation cost in the mod.
Need both hands for the airway? Bring the ACCUVAC tip into the mouth and press Middle mouse to park it for SALAD. It continues suction while you use the laryngoscope and tube. Press Middle mouse again to remove it. The manual bag cannot be parked.

Manual suction bag

Left mouse gives one squeeze. Keep the tip in the mouth and click again after each squeeze completes. Holding the button does not repeat squeezes. Watch the collection bag fill; a full bag stops drawing.

Needle decompression

Examine the patient before placing a needle. An unnecessary decompression can create a simple pneumothorax. Continued progression depends on the air leak, available drainage, positive pressure and altitude changes.

A decompression outlet can relieve pressure while the underlying leak remains. Reassess after placement and if the patient worsens. Repeated needles should prompt a check of the diagnosis, catheter function and need for more durable drainage.

Addon Options > ACM Extended: Trauma > Procedures > Allow Needle Decompression controls new needle placements. The procedure minigame and available equipment can add their own requirements.

Chest seals and thoracostomy

Cover the actual chest wound with the seal and inspect the other surface for additional wounds. Seal placement and peeling change the patient’s chest-seal state. A seal does not remove pleural blood or clear blast lung.

Chest seal

Purpose: cover the actual open chest wound.

Recheck: seal position, other wounds and the patient’s response.

Thoracostomy

Purpose: create a pleural opening for drainage.

Recheck: opening, remaining air leak and any change after closure.

Chest tube

Purpose: maintain a drainage route, including passive pleural blood drainage.

Recheck: tube position, drainage and breathing.

Finger thoracostomy and a chest tube create different drainage conditions. The thoracostomy minigame requires site preparation, incision and completion of the opening. Inadequate preparation can seed infection. A placed tube can passively drain pleural blood, with a configured base rate of 0.12 L/min.

Closing or sealing a thoracostomy tract changes its drainage status. Reassess after closure, tube adjustment or a change in positive pressure. The chest-seal tray action on a completed thoracostomy routes through the same clinical closure function as closing the tract.

Endotracheal tube checks

Confirm the tube’s position and reassess after moving it. A tube that advances into one main bronchus reduces the lung available for ventilation; the model reduces effective compliance and oxygenation. A partially placed obstructing tube can produce a flat capnograph.

Deflate the cuff before withdrawing the tube. Pulling repeatedly against an inflated cuff can cause airway injury. Sedation and paralysis have separate effects: rocuronium does not provide sedation or analgesia. Use the medication cards and ketamine pupil finding together.

Capnography: read the shape

A capnograph shows CO2 leaving the airway throughout each breath. Read the shape, rate and trend together with the patient’s pulse, chest movement and delivered breaths.

Normal plateau, example CO2 level 40 mmHg and breathing rate 12/min.

Normal plateau

Recognize

A quick rise, a gently rising plateau, then a sharp return toward zero.

In the game

The default shape when neither obstruction nor unresolved ventilator effort is selecting another pattern. A normal shape can still have a low or high CO2 value.

Check next

Compare the CO2 trend with breathing rate, delivered volume and perfusion. The shape alone does not establish adequate circulation or oxygenation.

Obstruction / shark fin

Recognize

A slower, slanted rise and a less distinct plateau. Increase obstruction severity to see the fin become more pronounced.

In the game

Blood or vomit obstructing the airway, or a partially placed tube, can select this pattern. A separate bronchospasm diagnosis is not required by the current waveform selector.

Check next

Reassess airway contents and tube position, use suction where indicated, and check the delivered breaths. Recheck the waveform after correcting the obstruction.

Curare cleft

Recognize

A notch interrupts the plateau as the patient makes an effort that is not coordinated with the ventilator. Increasing the example effort makes the notch deeper and wider.

In the game

Unresolved respiratory effort or coughing while the ventilator is driving breaths can create a cleft. An awake patient who is completely paralyzed cannot create a notch through awareness alone.

Check next

Reassess patient effort, ventilator coordination and the ongoing sedation and paralysis plan. A cleft is not a direct measurement of sedation; rocuronium provides paralysis without sedation or analgesia.

Flat trace

Recognize

No exhaled CO2 waveform. This example stays at zero regardless of the selected rate or peak.

In the game

An inserted but incompletely seated, obstructing ETT forces the flat shape. No breathing or no effective exhaled CO2 also removes the waveform.

Check next

Check the airway and device position, chest movement, actual delivered breaths and circulation immediately. A flat trace has more than one possible cause.

EMMA display reference

Waveform shape, waveform height and the breath-averaged number answer different questions. Allow the number to settle after a change and reassess the patient when the shape changes.

Positive pressure and circulation

Positive pressure can reduce estimated stroke volume when circulating volume is low. Both active BVM ventilation and the ventilator participate. Higher PEEP can increase the penalty. Treat hemorrhage and inadequate ventilation together, then reassess pressure and pulse after starting support.
