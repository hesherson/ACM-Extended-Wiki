Airway And Chest

Decompression, seals, drainage and tubes. The part of the addon where doing the wrong thing confidently causes the injury you were treating for.

The one thing

A needle costs something. Decompressing a chest that did not need it seeds the pneumothorax you were looking for, and a persistent leak can later progress toward {{tension-pneumothorax|tension}} when drainage is inadequate.

01

Tension Pneumothorax & Needle Decompression

The action is available on any living casualty at any time, rather than only when a tension is present, so the decision comes from what you find on examination rather than from a label the game has already applied.

Before the needle goes in, the indication is checked. If a tension or simple pneumothorax is present, it works as expected. If not, you have just made a hole in a healthy chest.

Addon Options → ACM Extended: Trauma → Procedures → Allow Needle Decompression controls new needle placements. There is no separate visible option in the current build to disable the consequences of unnecessary decompression.

Why this is in the addonTeaching intent

Most medical sims make needle decompression a free action, which trains the habit of needling anything that looks unwell. That habit is actively dangerous, and it is one of the more common criticisms of prehospital chest decompression in the real literature.

Making it cost something turns it into a decision. You are meant to examine first, and continued leakage can worsen the casualty after the procedure. The current model does not use a fixed 90-second iatrogenic-tension timer.

It is recoverable. A simple pneumothorax you caused can be decompressed and drained like any other, so the cost is time and a lesson rather than a dead casualty.

02

Positive Pressure & Venous Return

Ventilating a casualty raises intrathoracic pressure, which opposes venous return. In a full tank that costs nothing measurable. In an empty one it costs a great deal.

Volume stateStroke volume penalty
Fullabout 0%
30% to 40% volume loss, PEEP-5 referenceabout 9.7% to 13.3% of remaining estimated SV
Configured base maximum35%; higher PEEP can increase this penalty up to 1.5×

High PEEP multiplies it up to 1.5 times. This applies to both the ventilator and manual bagging, so an enthusiastic BVM technique on a shocked casualty does real harm.

Address hemorrhage and inadequate oxygenation together, and reassess perfusion after starting ventilation or changing PEEP. See the positive-pressure graph for the current game calculation.

03

Capnography: EtCO2 & Waveform

The EMMA reads {{etco2|EtCO2}} at the tube and shows a number, a rate, and a rolling trace. The number is a breath to breath trailing average rather than a single breath, so it settles rather than jumping, and a change in it means something has actually changed.

InventoryEMMA mainstream capnograph, inline on the tube

Read the shape, not only the number. A square trace with a flat top is a patient moving air. A trace that slopes upward instead of levelling is obstruction. A trace that shrinks breath by breath while the ventilator settings have not changed is a lung getting stiffer, and in pressure control that is the only warning you get.

Value falling with a pulse present usually means circulation, not ventilation. Cardiac output carries CO2 to the lung, so a dropping number on unchanged settings points at the pump before it points at the tube.

Red Flags

  • Needling on suspicion alone. Examine first. A wrong needle creates the injury.
  • Repeat needles when the first did not help. Reassess the diagnosis, catheter function and need for further drainage.
  • Ventilating an empty casualty. You are trading oxygenation for cardiac output.
  • High PEEP in shock. Multiplies the {{preload|preload}} penalty by half again.
