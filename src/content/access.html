IV access & infusions

Compare gauges, choose an access site, calculate a drip, and check why a bag is draining slowly or delivering less fluid than expected.

A larger target multiplier makes the peripheral IV minigame more forgiving. A lower gauge number means a larger bore. Vein caliber, depth, movement and gauge suitability modify the target further. A catheter larger than the vein’s configured limit adds a placement penalty.

18G uses the initialized 0.50 flow factor. The flow function contains a 0.55 fallback for an absent setting; that fallback is not the normal initialized value.

02

Pressure and site difficulty

SiteLow end of peripheral patencyFull patency

Between the endpoints, patency increases linearly. Low patency reduces the feel radius, hit radius and maximum palpation indicator intensity. It does not reduce the minigame hit radius to zero. The vein’s own geometry still matters at full patency.

At the default EJ settings, target radius grows from 0.0045 at 14G to 0.0075 at 20G, expressed as a fraction of the body image width. These are minigame geometry values.

05

Fluid loss at a compromised site

Site conditionFraction of drained fluid admitted to circulation
Intact site100%
Native stage-2 infiltration80%
Compromised catheter flag20%
Stage-2 infiltration and compromised flag together16%
IOThis IV leakage calculation returns 100%

Fractions multiply. A bag can lose 100 mL while only 16 mL reaches circulation in the combined case. Infused medication uses the same split: the systemic dose follows admitted volume, and the remainder is recorded as local leakage. See fluid compartments and conversion for what happens after fluid enters circulation.
