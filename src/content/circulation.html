ACM EXTENDED / REFERENCE

Cardiac rhythms

Recognition, causes, entry patterns, treatment responses and transition timing for the rhythms implemented in ACM Extended.

Open printable cardiac rhythm chart

Confirm the pulse

Use the ECG together with a pulse check, blood pressure, breathing and perfusion. PEA has an electrical rhythm without a pulse. VT may be perfusing or pulseless. Torsades is pulseless immediately, including its narrower entry pattern.

For the relationship between rhythm, filling, stroke volume, vasoconstriction and systemic vascular resistance, see circulation during hemorrhage and cardiology and oxygen delivery.

How to read these monitor examples

Each strip spans six seconds and uses the mod’s ECG shapes, sampling interval and bright green monitor color. The examples hold the electrical rate steady; AFib uses variable intervals. Pulse status must be assessed separately. Motion, compressions and poor electrode contact can change the live display.

The vertical scale shows relative monitor deflection, with upward deflections positive. It is not a calibrated millivolt scale. The torsades strip shows its established appearance; the rhythm’s entry transition is described below.

02

Sinus rhythm

Pulse: Present if perfusion is adequate

Organized P-QRS-T pattern at slower rates. At faster rates the monitor uses a compressed narrow complex pattern.

ReferenceWhat happens in game
RateFollows the patient's actual heart rate
Entry / transient rhythmAppears directly on recovery or when an induced perfusing rhythm terminates. There is no separate timed entry rhythm.
Common causesNormal rhythm after stabilization. Pain, blood loss, oxygenation, temperature and medications can move the rate while it remains sinus.
What to doAssess perfusion, breathing and injury. A normal looking ECG does not prove normal blood volume or adequate ventilation. No shock is indicated for sinus rhythm.
Conversion / deteriorationChanges as physiology changes. If the rate becomes critically high or low, or pressure/oxygenation fails, the native deterioration rules can take over.
03

Sinus bradycardia

Pulse: May be weak; check directly

Organized sinus complexes with longer R-R intervals. This is a rate presentation of sinus rhythm, not a separate selectable electrical rhythm.

ReferenceWhat happens in game
RateSlow sinus rate; below 60 BPM is a descriptive band
Entry / transient rhythmRate slows progressively according to the cause. There is no fixed countdown or separate entry waveform.
Common causesHypoxia, hypothermia, drug depression/toxicity and late circulatory failure. A head injury with a Cushing response can also slow the rate.
What to doCorrect oxygenation/ventilation and assess pressure, temperature and medication exposure. Atropine can raise rate; identify whether the underlying cause needs reversal, rewarming or another intervention.
Conversion / deteriorationBelow 40 BPM outside an existing critical vitals grace period, the game enters its fatal rate pathway. VF is the usual electrical selection; active adenosine can instead select asystole. This is game behavior, not a requirement to wait for that threshold.
04

Sinus tachycardia

Pulse: Present until perfusion fails

Regular narrow complexes, with less room between beats. P and T detail compresses at high rates.

ReferenceWhat happens in game
RateFast sinus rate; above 100 BPM is a descriptive band
Entry / transient rhythmTracks the patient's changing rate. Hemorrhagic tachycardia does not automatically become SVT under the default settings.
Common causesBlood loss, pain, hypoxia, physiological stress, epinephrine or distress during inadequate sedation/paralysis.
What to doControl hemorrhage, replace volume, treat pain, support ventilation and reassess any pressor or sedative delivery. Adenosine does not treat the underlying sinus response.
Conversion / deteriorationA sustained pressor surge can induce atrial tachycardia or SVT. A native rate above 220 BPM can enter VT with a pulse and then the critical vitals pathway.
05

Atrial fibrillation

Pulse: Usually present

Irregularly irregular narrow complexes with a fibrillatory baseline and no consistent P wave.

ReferenceWhat happens in game
RateControlled target about 90 BPM
Entry / transient rhythmCan be induced directly or follow rate control of AFib with RVR. The rhythm label changes immediately when treatment is effective; the actual heart rate can settle progressively. No separate timed entry stage is drawn.
Common causesRate controlled AFib with RVR, or an instructor created scenario. Severe hypothermia can initiate the faster RVR presentation.
What to doFollow pulse, pressure and symptoms. Correct cold exposure and other physiological problems. Synchronized cardioversion can convert this modeled rhythm to sinus when intervention is appropriate.
Conversion / deteriorationRate control does not itself restore sinus rhythm. Successful synchronized cardioversion restores sinus; native critical deterioration overrides the perfusing rhythm.
06

Atrial fibrillation with RVR

Pulse: Present, with impaired perfusion possible

Fast, irregularly irregular narrow complex rhythm. Uneven R-R intervals and a fibrillatory baseline distinguish it from regular SVT.

ReferenceWhat happens in game
RateDefault target about 150 BPM
Entry / transient rhythmStarts directly when induced. At core temperature 32°C or lower there is a 15% chance on each eligible cooling check; this is probabilistic, so there is no fixed onset time.
Common causesSevere hypothermia and instructor induction. The modeled rhythm adds a 28 mmHg pressure penalty and can produce discomfort and brief obtundation.
What to doRewarm and assess perfusion. Esmolol or amiodarone can rate control it to AFib. Synchronized cardioversion can restore sinus. Adenosine does not terminate AFib.
Conversion / deteriorationRate control targets AFib near 90 BPM after enough medication effect develops. Successful cardioversion restores sinus immediately, followed by the short post-shock display period. Ongoing native deterioration can still produce arrest.
07

Atrial tachycardia

Pulse: Present

Regular fast narrow complexes. The small atrial deflection is easiest to see when the rate leaves enough space between complexes.

ReferenceWhat happens in game
RateDefault target about 185 BPM
Entry / transient rhythmCan be induced directly, or after about 6 seconds of a sustained qualifying pressor surge. The rhythm appears directly once induced; no additional timed entry pattern follows.
Common causesExcess epinephrine related drive or stacked measured push dose support, with the patient already tachycardic. It adds a 12 mmHg pressure penalty and discomfort.
What to doCheck medication concentration and actual infusion rate. Esmolol or amiodarone can terminate it; synchronized cardioversion is available. Adenosine's dedicated conversion effect is limited to SVT.
Conversion / deteriorationSuccessful medication conversion or cardioversion returns to sinus. Reducing the original surge is still necessary, because conversion alone does not remove the cause.
08

Supraventricular tachycardia (SVT)

Pulse: Present, may be poorly perfusing

Regular rapid narrow complexes, generally without a clearly separated P wave. It should not look like the broad complexes of VT.

ReferenceWhat happens in game
RateDefault target about 190 BPM
Entry / transient rhythmDirect induction, or about 6 seconds of a stronger qualifying pressor surge. There is no separate timed narrow to wide entry sequence. The old optional rate only trigger is disabled by default and is not an exposed Addon Option.
Common causesA sufficiently strong epinephrine/push dose surge or instructor induction. The rhythm adds an 18 mmHg pressure penalty and can cause discomfort or obtundation.
What to doRapid IV / IO adenosine can terminate the modeled regular SVT. Complete the required flush when enabled. Esmolol and amiodarone are additional modeled conversion paths; synchronized cardioversion can restore sinus.
Conversion / deteriorationA qualifying adenosine dose attempts conversion after about 1 second of systemic effect. It briefly slows rate without deliberately creating asystole. Other drug conversions depend on active effect rather than a fixed bag hanging timer.
09

Ventricular tachycardia with a pulse

Pulse: Present; verify before choosing shock mode

Regular broad ventricular complexes with little normal P-QRS-T separation.

ReferenceWhat happens in game
RateUses the patient's actual heart rate
Entry / transient rhythmThe native fatal rate pathway selects VT when heart rate exceeds 220 BPM outside a critical grace period. No separate timed entry morphology is implemented for this rhythm.
Common causesExtreme physiological or drug related tachycardia. The underlying circulatory condition determines whether the patient remains perfusing.
What to doUse synchronized cardioversion for an unstable perfusing VT. IV / IO lidocaine has a conversion path in the current mod. Correct the cause of the extreme rate. The AED's automatic analysis does not advise defibrillation for this perfusing VT.
Conversion / deteriorationCritical deterioration can lead to pulseless VT or another arrest rhythm. The native recovery check can clear VT when heart rate is above 45 and below 200 BPM and MAP is above 60 and below 180 mmHg. There is no extra fixed six second recovery hold in the current observer.
10

Pulseless ventricular tachycardia

Pulse: Absent

Broad, rapid ventricular complexes. The waveform alone does not establish whether a pulse exists.

ReferenceWhat happens in game
RateDisplayed electrical rate generally 200–240 BPM
Entry / transient rhythmSelected at arrest or after deterioration from a critical fast rhythm. There is no separate timed entry waveform.
Common causesDeterioration of severe tachycardia, critical injury or an induced arrest scenario.
What to doCPR, effective ventilation, defibrillation with SYNC off, and correction of reversible causes. Amiodarone and IV / IO lidocaine can improve defibrillation response.
Conversion / deteriorationWithout effective CPR, it can deteriorate to VF and then asystole through randomized checks. Successful defibrillation still requires the patient's ROSC conditions to be met. Repeating a native VF/pulseless VT shock within 60 seconds of the previous recorded shock can instead cause asystole in the current game.
11

Ventricular fibrillation

Pulse: Absent

Continuous chaotic deflections without organized QRS complexes. The electrical display can vary roughly 100–220 BPM, but this is not a pulse.

ReferenceWhat happens in game
RateNo organized ventricular rate; the monitor may show an electrical number
Entry / transient rhythmAppears directly at arrest or after deterioration from pulseless VT. No separate timed coarse to fine entry sequence is implemented.
Common causesCritical injury, severe physiological deterioration, progression of another arrest rhythm, or an unsynchronized shock to an organized perfusing rhythm.
What to doCPR, ventilation and defibrillation with SYNC off. Check blood volume, oxygenation and chest causes. Amiodarone/lidocaine can improve the modeled shock response.
Conversion / deteriorationCan deteriorate to asystole when CPR is absent and randomized deterioration checks succeed. A successful shock does not guarantee ROSC if circulation remains inadequate.
12

Torsades de pointes

Pulse: Absent from the moment it is induced

Polymorphic ventricular complexes with changing amplitude and polarity, forming the characteristic twisting pattern.

ReferenceWhat happens in game
RateDefault electrical target about 220 BPM
Entry / transient rhythmStarts with a narrower, SVT like rapid pattern. Every fourth beat becomes more ventricular early, then the whole run gradually widens and develops its twisting amplitude. The default entry transition lasts 15.84 seconds, equivalent to three 5.28-second monitor sweeps.
Common causesMarked hypocalcemia, raised ICP or cumulative amiodarone exposure of at least 2.2 g. The trigger is probabilistic, including the severe tier; it is not guaranteed to fire immediately. It can also be induced for training.
What to doTreat as pulseless arrest throughout the entry transition: CPR, effective ventilation and defibrillation with SYNC off. Magnesium can terminate it once sufficient active effect develops. Correct low calcium or raised ICP and stop excessive amiodarone exposure.
Conversion / deteriorationMagnesium suppresses recurrence while enough active effect remains. After magnesium assisted conversion there is a short default 20-second recurrence protection period; defibrillation uses a separate short protection period. The underlying cause can re-induce torsades after protection/effect fades. Amiodarone does not treat this modeled rhythm.
13

Pulseless electrical activity (PEA)

Pulse: Absent despite organized ECG activity

Organized abnormal complexes with beat to beat variation. The monitor's BPM is an electrical rate and does not establish mechanical circulation.

ReferenceWhat happens in game
RateElectrical display varies within 60–100 BPM
Entry / transient rhythmAppears directly when reversible circulatory failure selects PEA. There is no separate fixed bradycardic entry rhythm in the current build.
Common causesSevere blood loss, severe hypoxia, tension pneumothorax or a large hemothorax. The ECG alone does not identify which cause is present.
What to doCPR, effective ventilation and rapid correction of the cause. Control bleeding and restore circulating volume; treat obstructive chest injury. Do not defibrillate PEA.
Conversion / deteriorationEligibility is rechecked every 5 seconds. Corrected PEA can attempt ROSC without waiting for a shock. After 6 minutes, the reversible PEA handling expires and the general arrest selection resumes; this does not guarantee a particular next rhythm.
14

Asystole

Pulse: Absent

Near flat baseline with small monitor noise; no organized ventricular complexes.

ReferenceWhat happens in game
Rate0 BPM
Entry / transient rhythmCan appear directly with profound blood loss or another severe arrest cause, or after VF deterioration. No separate timed entry waveform is implemented.
Common causesProfound blood loss, progressive untreated arrest, severe lidocaine toxicity or an inappropriate/refractory shock. Blood volume below 3.1 L can select asystole in the arrest handler.
What to doCPR, effective ventilation and correction of reversible causes. Defibrillation is not indicated. Use actual pulse and circulation findings to assess recovery.
Conversion / deteriorationA flatline alone does not determine whether the unit is dead. Recovery requires a living patient and successful resuscitation with adequate circulation; dead units cannot regain medication effects or ROSC.

Transition timing

TransitionDefault timingHow to interpret it
Pressor surge > atrial tachycardia or SVTAbout 6 s after a sustained qualifying surgeThe patient must already be tachycardic and perfusing. A stronger surge selects SVT. A 90-second refractory period limits repeated automatic induction.
Cold patient > AFib with RVRRandom eligible cooling checks at ≤32°C15% per eligible cooling check. No fixed delay can be promised.
Torsades entry > developed twisting pattern15.84 sA visual transition across three monitor sweeps. Arrest begins immediately, so do not wait for the broad twisting pattern before treating.
Adenosine > SVT conversion attemptAbout 1 s after effective rapid deliveryFor a 6 mg reference push delivered over 5 s, conversion strength is sufficient in this model. Required line flush and actual delivery determine when the clock starts.
Esmolol > rate control/conversionDose- and delivery dependentA full reference IV dose reaches the current rate control threshold at roughly 4.6 s; its reference peak is 20 s. An infusion reaches that effect later.
Amiodarone > atrial rhythm responseDose- and delivery dependentA full reference IV dose reaches the AFib-RVR/atrial tach response threshold at roughly 2.6 s and the SVT threshold at roughly 5 s. These are modeled effect thresholds, not a delay from connecting a bag.
Magnesium > torsades termination/suppressionDose- and delivery dependentFor a fully delivered 2 g reference: about 21 s to termination effect, about 15 s to suppression effect, 90 s to peak. Conversion still requires ROSC eligibility.
Critical vitals > worsening instabilityInitial grace 15–30 s; persistent severe values can cause arrest after roughly 30–45 sChecks run every 1 s. Very extreme rate/pressure or a nonperfusing rhythm can end the grace sooner.
Pulseless VT > VF > asystole without CPRRandom checks every 15–30 sA check must also pass a randomized 30–60 s minimum since deterioration gate and the deterioration chance. This is not a fixed per rhythm countdown.
Reversible PEA > ROSC checkEvery 5 s, up to a 6 min handling windowCorrection can produce an earlier attempt. Expiry returns to general arrest selection, rather than guaranteeing asystole.
Shock > live monitor/pulse displayAbout 2 sThe short post-shock display is shared by defibrillation/cardioversion. It is separate from the 60 s native repeat shock consequence.

Conversion changes the rhythm state when the treatment succeeds. The monitor then displays the new pattern as it redraws. Heart rate can settle over time, so electrical conversion and normalization of rate or pressure are not the same event.

Critical vitals progression

Finding during the critical periodCurrent behavior
Heart rate >245 or <10 BPM, or MAP >220 or <30 mmHgCan trigger arrest on the next 1-second critical vitals check.
Heart rate >240 or <30 BPM, or MAP >200 or <55 mmHgIf still present beyond the initial 15–30 s grace plus 15 s, the critical handler can trigger arrest.
Heart rate >45 and <200 BPM, MAP >60 and <180 mmHg, no arrestThe critical handler can resolve; perfusing VT can return to sinus.
Severe hypoxia or class IV hemorrhageSeparate native pathways can cause arrest without waiting for the rate/pressure grace.
No effective circulation when the patient deterioratesPEA is selected. With circulation present, a rate above 200 BPM favors a pulseless VT target; lower rates favor VF. General arrest selection can still choose between VF and pulseless VT.

These boundaries explain the game’s transitions. They are not treatment targets to wait for. A patient can already be unstable well before crossing an arrest threshold.

Cardioversion and defibrillation

RhythmModeModeled response
AFib / AFib with RVRSYNC on90% conversion chance per synchronized shock.
Atrial tachycardia / SVTSYNC on97% conversion chance per synchronized shock.
VT with a pulseSYNC on90% fallback conversion chance. Check that the rhythm is perfusing.
TorsadesSYNC off95% chance to attempt conversion, followed by the shared ROSC check.
VF / pulseless VTSYNC offResponse depends on resistance, current amiodarone/lidocaine effect and CPR history. Current full lidocaine effect can make a conversion attempt certain; ROSC still requires adequate circulation.
Organized perfusing rhythm shocked with SYNC offWrong mode55% default chance of inducing VF. A lack of immediate VF does not make this the correct mode.
VF / pulseless VT / torsades with SYNC onWrong modeThe shock is blocked because there is no suitable R wave for synchronization.
Sinus, PEA or asystole shockedNo indicated shockThe current manual shock path can place the patient into asystolic arrest.

The short 2-second post-shock display period does not remove the separate native repeat shock rule. For VF/pulseless VT, a shock within 60 seconds of the previous recorded shock can select asystole. The current transaction records synchronized shocks too, so check recent treatment history before another shock.

Hemothorax not above 1.2 L in the native checkTreat the chest injury and blood loss.

Rhythm entry durations, per rhythm cardioversion percentages and automatic surge thresholds are current build defaults. They do not have corresponding end user sliders in this build. Use the exposed options above for mission tuning.

For a repeatable test, use the same patient, medication concentration, delivery rate and addon settings. Record the pulse, blood pressure, displayed rhythm, time of systemic medication delivery, and time of conversion. For torsades, record arrest onset separately from the end of its 15.84-second entry pattern.
