Head injury

Assess pupils, support brain perfusion and ventilation, and recognize progressive herniation.

Assessment priorities

  1. Check response and reassess pupils. Record whether pupils are equal, reactive, sluggish or fixed.
  2. Control hemorrhage and assess blood pressure. Low circulating volume reduces the pressure available to perfuse the brain.
  3. Assess the airway and effective breathing. Support inadequate ventilation and reassess oxygenation.
  4. Follow trends after positioning, fluids, pressors, osmotherapy or ventilator changes.

Head impact and blast exposure can enter the TBI system. A visible head wound is not required for blast related TBI.

ICP and brain perfusion

Intracranial pressure (ICP) opposes the pressure supplying the brain. The model calculates cerebral perfusion pressure as:

CPPBrain perfusion pressure=MAPArterial driving pressure−ICPPressure inside the skull

Baseline ICP is 10 mmHg in the default configuration. Structural injury, reduced perfusion, inadequate ventilation, fluid overload, positive pressure and ventilator dyssynchrony can raise it. The configured structural ceiling is scaled by injury severity; 40 mmHg is not an absolute cap on all combined effects.

The structural rise coefficient is 0.015 per second and the configured maximum CO2-related coefficient is 0.12 per second. Each is multiplied by its own physiological inputs and the result is rate limited. The configured global rise cap is 0.025 mmHg per second and the fall cap is 0.04 mmHg per second; direct procedure effects can be applied separately. Individual contributions vary with the patient.

Stage 0–2 TBI vital targets are limited so TBI alone does not cross the fatal HR, MAP or sustained severe hypoventilation thresholds. This does not protect the patient from simultaneous blood loss, hypoxia or another arrest cause.

Positioning, treatment and transport

In the current game implementation, elevated head positioning blocks new herniation progression and allows the cascade to recede. It also reduces the MAP used by the brain calculation by a default 6 mmHg, so reassess perfusion. This is a specific game mechanic.

Hypertonic saline or mannitol can reduce pressure through osmotherapy. Address inadequate volume and blood pressure as well. Excessive ventilation can lower ICP while adding ischemic injury in the model; increasing ventilation indefinitely does not keep improving the patient.

When the vehicle evacuation option is enabled, entering a vehicle marks the patient as evacuated for the herniation calculation and pauses cascade progression. This does not erase established injury or stop every other physiological system. Continue monitoring during transport.

Progression timing

At default settings, ICP at or above 30 mmHg accumulates time toward a 600-second herniation gate while the head is not elevated. Below that pressure, or with the head elevated, the accumulated time falls at half speed. Once the gate is open, progression uses 360-second stage intervals. Pressure control, elevated head positioning and evacuation can interrupt or pause this progression. This timing does not protect against another cause of deterioration.
