ACM EXTENDED / REFERENCE

Head injury assessment

Follow neurological findings together with perfusion and breathing.

01

Recognize deterioration

Reassess consciousness, pupils, breathing and circulation after head injury. Rising ICP can produce a Cushing response with increased vascular resistance, pressure support and a slower heart rate. Exhausted autonomic control can later make tone unstable or predominantly dilated; the full Cushing pattern does not have to appear first. Medication effects can also change consciousness or pupils, so review treatment history.

Injury history

Structural severity records the high-water injury severity. Severe and critical injuries retain reduced cerebrovascular reserve even after active symptoms settle.

Active burden

Acute severity can fall toward zero in a stable mild or moderate injury. ICP returns toward its normal baseline, and a resolved concussion no longer continues generating deterioration.

Check recovery conditions

Assess oxygen delivery, ventilation, ICP and perfusion together. Severe structural injury adds a CPP minimum of 55 mmHg; critical injury adds 60 mmHg. A normal SpO₂ reading does not replace missing blood flow.

The clinical descriptor reference explains examination wording. See Traumatic brain injury for the broader system guide.
